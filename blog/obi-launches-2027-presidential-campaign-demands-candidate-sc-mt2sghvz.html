OBI LAUNCHES 2027 PRESIDENTIAL CAMPAIGN, DEMANDS CANDIDATE SCRUTINY

Fri, 21 Aug 2026 10:10 GMT • Credit: Punch • D-Ray Show Blog News

Former Anambra governor Peter Obi formally began his bid for the 2027 Nigerian presidency on Friday, holding a press conference at his Onitsha residence. In the remarks, Obi urged all presidential hopefuls to step out from behind “high walls of power” and present themselves directly to the electorate, defending their records and answering questions about their capacity to govern.

Obi, who is running with Senator Rabiu Kwankwaso as his vice‑presidential candidate, emphasized that his campaign will be conducted personally across Nigeria, without reliance on proxies or surrogates. He said the people deserve to hear directly from those seeking their mandate and to evaluate each candidate’s credentials, policy proposals and past performance.

The candidate highlighted a litany of challenges confronting the nation, including poverty, food insecurity, internal displacement, unemployment, weak infrastructure, unreliable electricity, and pervasive insecurity. Obi argued that Nigeria possesses the human capital, resources and market potential to overcome these hurdles, but only if leadership embraces presence, empathy and direct engagement with affected communities.

Obi outlined a development agenda focused on shifting the economy from consumption‑driven to production‑driven growth. Priorities include boosting agriculture through better security, irrigation and market access; expanding manufacturing and agro‑processing; investing in technology, construction, energy and creative industries; and improving education, vocational training, health services and maternal‑child care. He also mapped regional strengths, urging each zone to leverage its comparative advantages while contributing to a unified national economy.

The former governor called for an issue‑based campaign free of ethnic, religious or partisan propaganda, urging voters to scrutinise promises and demand evidence. He pledged that his campaign will centre on Nigerians’ urgent concerns and that the election outcome should be respected by all sides. Obi’s team plans to tour the country’s regions in the coming weeks, engaging directly with citizens to test the appeal of his “presence and empathy” narrative.

What remains to be seen is how rival candidates will respond to Obi’s challenge and whether voters will prioritize direct accountability over traditional political structures. The race is set to intensify as the 2027 election approaches, with each contender positioning themselves on the nation’s pressing economic and security agenda.
